How to use LeafGuard

Three steps

  1. Press Upload photo and pick a photo of one tomato leaf, filling most of the frame.
  2. Press Check this leaf. The answer comes back in about a second.
  3. Look at the coloured label.

What the colours mean

  • Healthy The leaf looks healthy.
  • Disease found The disease name is shown below the label. Read What to do.
  • Not sure The photo was not clear enough. Take a new, clearer photo.

Taking a good photo

  • Use daylight. Flash and deep shade both confuse the model.
  • Photograph a single leaf against a plain background.
  • Hold the camera steady. A blurry photo is much more likely to come back as uncertain.
  • Get close enough that the leaf fills most of the picture.

What the result means

The confidence is how strongly the model favours that one class over the others. A high score means the photo looks like the training examples of that disease. It is not a probability that the diagnosis is correct.

When the confidence is low, or the photo does not look like a leaf, LeafGuard shows Not sure and asks for a better photo instead of guessing.

If something goes wrong

  • "That file type is not supported": choose a JPG, PNG or WEBP photo. Screenshots of documents or PDFs will not work.
  • "That image is larger than 5 MB": most phones have a setting for a smaller photo size, or send the photo to yourself to shrink it.
  • "That image is only ... pixels": the photo is too small to read. Take it closer, or use the original instead of a thumbnail.
  • "The diagnosis service is starting up": wait a minute and press Check again. The footer shows the model version once it is ready.

Limitations worth knowing

  • It was trained on laboratory photographs: single leaves on plain backgrounds. Photos taken in a real field are harder for it.
  • It only knows the diseases listed above. Anything else gets matched to the closest one it does know, which is why the uncertain state matters.
  • It looks at one leaf at a time and knows nothing about the rest of the plant.
  • It is a student project for learning the lifecycle of an AI system, not a certified diagnostic tool. Confirm with an expert before treating a crop.
